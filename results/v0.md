# v0: a tiny model that learns which zone drivers visit next

Run on 2026-11-11. Every number here comes from `results/v0.json` (and `results/v0_shortest_val.json` for the shortest route). Scored on the **300 validation routes only**. The 50 pilot routes were never used for training, tuning or scoring.

## Results in one paragraph

Lower is better; 0 = the driver's exact order. On the 300 val routes the plain shortest route scores **0.0898 [0.0840, 0.0959]**. The tiny zone model without history (**v0**, High+Medium, beam 5) scores **0.0351 [0.0316, 0.0388]** and beats the shortest route on 290 of 300 routes. Adding station history (**v0-hist**) brings it to **0.0229–0.0238**. The strict "only the past" version (**v0-hist-past**) scores **0.0255 [0.0222, 0.0292]**. **The catch:** v0 without history gets the *direction* of the route right only about half the time, and the official score hardly punishes a reversed route. Its good score therefore partly reflects the metric, not real knowledge of where drivers start. History fixes most of that (see "Direction").

### Direction: the metric hides a weakness in v0

The official score's sequence part only checks which stops are next to each other, not the direction (the exact reverse of the driver's route scores 0; see `results/plain_baseline.md`). So we checked direction separately (`v0_decode.py direction`). Kendall tau compares our zone order with the driver's: +1 = same order, −1 = exactly reversed, 0 = unrelated.

| Variant (beam 5) | Mean tau | Routes mostly backwards (tau < 0) | Exact zone order | Exact reverse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0, High | +0.10 | 139 | 43 | 27 |
| v0, High+Medium | +0.13 | 125 | 46 | 30 |
| v0-hist, High+Medium | +0.74 | 22 | 113 | 1 |
| v0-hist-past, High+Medium | +0.66 | 32 | 104 | 5 |
| Nearest zone control | +0.01 | 146 | 0 | 1 |

**v0 without history is close to a coin flip on direction.** It learns "which zones are next to each other in the driver's order", which is what the metric rewards, but not where the driver starts. History mostly fixes this: past routes at the same station show where drivers begin. Any claim like "the model predicts the driver's order" should be made with history models, or with this table next to it.

## The idea in one picture

A route has about 150 stops. Amazon has already grouped them into about 20 **zones** (IDs like `A-2.3B`). Drivers almost never leave a zone and come back later (0.96 revisits per route, against 14.5 for the shortest route; `results/sealed/zone_revisits.md`). So a route is mostly two decisions:

1. **Which order to do the zones in.** This is what drivers know and the shortest-route solver does not. The model learns it.
2. **Which order to do the stops inside each zone.** Zones are small (about 7 stops), so "shortest path" is a good guess. OR-Tools does it.

```
station -> [zone A-2.2A: 4 stops] -> [zone A-2.1A: 2 stops] -> [zone A-1.1A: 2 stops] -> ... -> station
            \___ model picks this order ___/   \___ OR-Tools orders the stops inside each box ___/
```

## What the model sees

At every step the driver is standing in one zone (at the start: the station) and has finished some zones. Every unfinished zone is a **candidate**. The model describes each candidate with a list of numbers called **features**:

| Group | Features (33 in v0) | Why it might help |
|---|---|---|
| The zone itself | position from the station (km east, km north, distance), number of stops, packages, share of stops with a delivery time window, earliest window end, total service time | far zones may be done last; urgent zones first |
| Relative to here | km east/north/away from the current zone | drivers move to nearby zones |
| Travel time | minutes from the current zone, minutes back to the station, time compared with the nearest candidate, rank by time, "is it the nearest?" | the solver's whole view of the world |
| Zone ID relations | same letter? same number? number gap; same sub-number? sub-number step (−3..+3); same sub-letter? sub-letter step; "ID neighbour" (one step away) | IDs encode Amazon's own sweep order: `A-1.2C` → `A-1.3C` → `A-1.3D` |
| Direction | does this move continue the last move's sub-number / sub-letter direction? | drivers sweep in one direction |
| Area progress | share of this zone's area (e.g. all of `A-1.*`) already done; is the current area unfinished? is the candidate in the current area? | drivers finish an area before leaving |
| Where we are | share of zones done, share left, number of zones | early and late moves differ |

Zone IDs, inspected on all 5,762 train routes: 115,516 of 115,518 (route, zone) pairs have the shape `letter-number.number letter` (e.g. `P-13.2C`); the other 2 (`LOA1`, `RTED3`) are treated as "no ID information". 6,164 of 849,357 stops (0.7%) have no zone and are attached to the zone of their nearest stop.

**v0-hist and v0-hist-past** see 18 more numbers per candidate, described below in "Station history".

## What the model predicts

One probability per candidate: "the driver goes here next". The probabilities add up to 1.

How it gets there, in three parts (`ZonePointer` in `scripts/v0_model.py`):

1. **Encoder.** Each candidate's feature list goes through a small two-layer network (33 → 64 → 64 numbers). Same weights for every candidate.
2. **One attention layer.** Each candidate looks at all the other candidates and mixes in what it sees (4 "heads", i.e. 4 different ways of looking). This lets a candidate know things like "I am the nearest one left" or "my area-mates are still open".
3. **Head.** 64 numbers → 1 score per candidate. **Softmax** turns scores into probabilities: p_i = e^(score_i) / Σ_j e^(score_j). Bigger score, bigger share; finished zones get probability 0.

It is called a **pointer network** because the answer "points" at one of the inputs rather than choosing from a fixed list of classes. Routes have different numbers of zones, and that is fine: it scores whatever candidates exist.

**Size: 27,329 parameters (v0) and 28,481 (with the 18 history features).** For scale: the brief allowed 20k–300k.

## How training works

- **Examples.** Walk along the driver's real zone order (the first time they entered each zone). Every step with at least 2 candidates is one example: the features of every candidate, and which one the driver actually chose. Steps with one candidate left teach nothing and are skipped.
- **Loss = cross-entropy.** For each example: −log(probability the model gave to the driver's real choice). If the model said 0.9, the loss is 0.105; if 0.1, it is 2.30. Confident wrong answers are punished hard. Training lowers the average.
- **Backprop and Adam.** For every weight, compute how the loss would change if the weight moved a little (the gradient), then move every weight a small step downhill. Adam is a version of "small step downhill" that adapts the step size per weight. Learning rate 0.002, 16 routes per step.
- **Standardising.** Each feature is shifted and scaled using the training data's mean and spread, so "minutes" and "yes/no" features start on a similar scale.
- **Early stopping.** 10% of the train routes (picked once, seeded) are held out. After every pass over the training data (an **epoch**) we measure the loss on the High-quality routes in that held-out 10% (val and the final test are High only). We keep the best epoch and stop after 5 epochs without improvement. This guards against **overfitting** (memorising training routes instead of learning habits).
- **Training data.** Train split only. "High" = High-quality routes only; "High+Medium" adds Medium. Low (102 routes) is never used.

## From probabilities to a full route (decoding)

- **Greedy:** at each step take the single most likely zone.
- **Beam search (beam 5):** keep the 5 best partial orders at every step; an order's score is the sum of its log-probabilities, i.e. the log of "probability of this whole order". This can undo a tempting early choice that leads somewhere bad.
- **Beam 5 + travel time:** the same, but every move also pays λ × (travel time of that move ÷ the route's typical zone-to-zone time). λ is the only tuned knob; it was picked from {0, 0.5, 1, 2} by the official score on the held-out High train routes (234 routes), never on val.
- **Inside each zone:** `stops_from_zone_order(route, zone_order, time_limit_s=None)` in `scripts/v0_decode.py`. OR-Tools finds the shortest path that starts at the last stop of the previous zone (the station for the first zone), visits the zone's stops once, and ends where leaving for the next zone is cheapest (an imaginary END node whose cost is the travel time to the next zone's nearest stop; the station after the last zone). Settings: PATH_CHEAPEST_ARC first solution + GREEDY_DESCENT local search (deterministic, milliseconds per zone). The same function is meant to be reused for other zone orders (for example Opus's).

Two reference rows that are not methods:
- **control/nearest_zone:** no model; always drive to the nearest unfinished zone, then the same in-zone solver. Shows how much the zone decomposition alone is worth.
- **oracle/driver_zone_order:** the driver's own zone order (uses the answer!) + the same in-zone solver. This is the ceiling for anything that only gets the zone order right.

## Station history (v0-hist and v0-hist-past)

The idea: zone names repeat day after day at the same station. If drivers from station DLA9 almost always go from `A-2.2A` to `A-2.1A`, a new DLA9 route containing both zones will probably do the same. The 2021 challenge winners got their biggest gain from history like this (copying zone precedence from one past route at the same station cut their score from 0.0487 to 0.0317, per their paper, arXiv 2112.15192).

From the train routes' zone orders we count (`scripts/v0_history.py`):

| Feature (18 total) | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| P(next = b \| now in a), same station and all stations pooled | "Of all the times past drivers left zone a, how often did they go straight to b?" Add-1 smoothing (pretend every candidate happened once more) so unseen moves get a small, not zero, probability; renormalised over the zones still open |
| raw counts behind those | so the model can tell "3 out of 4" from "300 out of 400" |
| share of past routes with a before b | not just "straight after" but "anywhere earlier" |
| how often b came before the other open zones | a candidate usually done before everything that is left is a good next pick |
| reference route | the past same-station route sharing the most zones with this one: is b in it, is the current zone in it, how far after the current zone b sits in its order, is b the next one in it, how much of today's route it covers |

Two leakage rules, two variants:
- **v0-hist (leave-one-out):** counts over all train routes; when building a train route's own features its own trips are subtracted, and it can never be its own reference. Val routes are not in the counts at all.
- **v0-hist-past (time-respecting):** a route only sees train routes dated **strictly before its own day**. Dates are whole days with no order within a day, so same-day routes are excluded too. This is the version a skeptic cannot attack with "you used the future".

Leakage checks (run, both passed with a maximum feature difference of exactly 0): for two train routes, leave-one-out features equal features computed from a table that never contained the route; and "past" features equal features from a table holding only earlier-dated routes.

**How much history each route gets** (`history_coverage` in `results/v0.json`). Train dates run from 2018-07-19 to 2018-08-26 (39 days).

| | Val, all-history | Val, past-only | Train, past-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routes | 300 | 300 | 5,762 |
| No same-station history at all | 0 | **4** | 53 |
| Fewer than 10 same-station routes | 0 | **17** | 276 |
| Median same-station routes available | 413 | 168.5 | 179.5 |
| Reference route covers < half the zones | 25 | 81 | 1,740 |
| Median share of zones the reference route shares | 0.91 | 0.78 | 0.78 |

Routes from the first days of the data get little or no past history; for them v0-hist-past falls back on the pooled counts (other stations) and the base features.

## A worked route

Val route `RouteID_00575ca4-8a63-49d2-96c8-9b347be5ba6c` (station DLA3, 2018-08-07, 59 stops, 15 zones). It is the median val route by the v0 (High+Medium, beam 5) score, so neither flattering nor unflattering for v0. The driver's zone order:

`P-2.3A → P-2.2A → P-2.1A → P-1.1A → P-1.2A → P-1.3A → P-1.2B → P-1.1B → P-1.1C → P-1.2C → P-1.3C → P-1.3D → P-1.2D → P-1.1D → O-1.1D`

The driver sweeps the `P-2` row downwards, then snakes through `P-1` (A column up, B column down, C up, D down), then finishes in `O-1.1D`. The model's top 3 at each of the first 5 steps, given the driver's real history (v0, High+Medium):

| Step | Driver is in | Model's top 3 (probability) | Driver chose | Model's probability for it (rank) | Nearest zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | station | P-1.2D 0.150, P-1.3A 0.146, O-1.1D 0.125 | P-2.3A | 0.026 (14th of 15) | O-1.1D |
| 2 | P-2.3A | **P-2.2A 0.905**, P-1.3A 0.024, P-1.1A 0.020 | P-2.2A | 0.905 (1st) | P-2.2A |
| 3 | P-2.2A | **P-2.1A 0.952**, P-1.1A 0.014, P-1.1B 0.010 | P-2.1A | 0.952 (1st) | P-2.1A |
| 4 | P-2.1A | **P-1.1A 0.889**, P-1.1C 0.041, P-1.1B 0.021 | P-1.1A | 0.889 (1st) | P-1.1C |
| 5 | P-1.1A | **P-1.2A 0.712**, P-1.1B 0.109, P-1.3A 0.105 | P-1.2A | 0.712 (1st) | P-1.1B |

Steps 4 and 5 are the interesting ones. The nearest zone is **not** the driver's choice, and the model still picks the driver's zone with high confidence: it has learned the ID pattern (same column, next sub-number), not just "go to the closest".

Step 1 is the weak spot: the model has no idea where the driver starts (14th of 15). Beam search then builds the **exact reverse** of the driver's zone order (`O-1.1D → P-1.1D → … → P-2.3A`). The route scores 0.0270, much better than the shortest route's 0.0600 on this route, but only because the metric forgives reversal.

The same route with history:
- **v0-hist** (High+Medium), step 1: P-1.3A 0.318, P-1.2D 0.123, P-1.2A 0.106; driver's P-2.3A gets 0.002 (14th). Steps 2–5: all correct but less confident (0.46, 0.67, 0.42, 0.37). Its beam order starts at P-1.3A and leaves the P-2 row to the end. Score 0.0476: worse than v0 on this route.
- **v0-hist-past** (High+Medium), step 1: P-1.3A 0.161, P-1.2B 0.106, P-1.2D 0.101; driver's P-2.3A gets 0.078 (5th). Steps 2–5: all correct (0.90, 0.98, 0.72, 0.69). Its beam order is **exactly the driver's zone order**. Score 0.0094, the same as the oracle on this route.

One route is an anecdote: v0-hist did worse than v0 here although it is better on average. Use it to see the mechanics, not to judge the variants.

## How to rerun

From `tacit-routes/`, with torch installed CPU-only (`VIRTUAL_ENV=.venv uv pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`):

```
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_decode.py shortest --workers 3          # shortest route on val (~22 min)
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_history.py                              # train-only history table (~45 s)
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag high --quality High      # v0, ~10 min
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag high_medium --quality High,Medium
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag hist_high --quality High --history all
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag hist_high_medium --quality High,Medium --history all
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag past_high --quality High --history past
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_model.py --tag past_high_medium --quality High,Medium --history past
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_decode.py decode --workers 3            # tune lambda, decode, score, write results/v0.json (~80 min)
.venv/bin/python -I scripts/v0_decode.py direction                     # Kendall tau table, added to results/v0.json
```

To turn any zone order into a full stop order (e.g. for Opus's zone orders), use the same function as v0:

```python
from v0_decode import load_npz, stops_from_zone_order
stops = stops_from_zone_order(load_npz(route_id), ["P-2.3A", "P-2.2A", ...], time_limit_s=None)
```

Outputs: checkpoints `runs/v0/<tag>/model.pt`, curves `runs/v0/<tag>/curve.csv` and `runs/v0/training_curve.svg`, every decoded order `runs/v0/val_orders.json`, finished variants cached in `runs/v0/cache/` (delete it to recompute). Torch uses 3 threads in training; decoding uses 3 worker processes with 1 thread each. Training is seeded but PyTorch on CPU is not guaranteed bit-identical across machines.

## Caveats

- **Direction.** See above: v0 without history often reverses the route, and the metric does not notice.
- **The split is random, not by date.** v0 and v0-hist are trained on routes dated after some val routes. For v0 that only means general habits; for v0-hist the history counts themselves include later days. v0-hist-past removes this for the history features; the model weights are still trained on all train dates. The frozen evaluation-side test set (later in time) would remove it completely.
- **v0-hist-past at training time**, early-dated train routes have little history, so the model learns partly from history-poor examples. That matches what it sees at val time.
- **λ was tuned on 234 held-out train routes.** Its effect on val is within noise, so it is not a real finding.
- **Six models and 18 val variants** were scored. The headline comparisons against the shortest route are so lopsided (≥ 277 of 300 routes) that multiple comparisons do not matter. The smaller family comparisons (past − all-history, p 0.02–0.55) should be read as descriptive.
- **The in-zone solver uses greedy descent, not a full search.** The oracle row (0.0072) shows this costs little.
- **No Opus comparison here.** Opus's pilot numbers are on the 50 pilot routes, not these 300. Do not put the two side by side.
- **Seeded but not bit-reproducible across machines** (PyTorch CPU kernels).
